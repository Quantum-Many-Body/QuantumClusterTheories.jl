QuantumClusterTheories.Conductivity — Type
Conductivity{B<:BrillouinZone}

The dc conductivity tensor task: the bubble approximation of the Kubo-Bastin formula with the permutation decomposition (Bonbien & Manchon, arXiv:2005.04678),

σ = σˢᵘʳᶠ + σˢᵉᵃ
σˢᵘʳᶠ_αα′ = (2π²e²/h)(1/SN)Σ_k ∫dω (-∂_ωf) Tr[v_α(k)A(k,ω)v_α′(k)A(k,ω)]       (symmetric, Fermi-surface)
σˢᵉᵃ_αα′ = (e²/2h)(1/SN)Σ_k ∫dω f(ω) Tr[{v_α∂_ω(Gʳ+Gᵃ)v_α′ - (α↔α′)}(Gʳ-Gᵃ)]   (antisymmetric, Fermi-sea)

with S the unitcell volume, N the number of momenta, A = (Gʳ-Gᵃ)/2πi the spectral matrix, G the periodized quantum cluster Green's function, and v_α(k) = ∂h₀(k)/∂k_α the bare velocity vertex obtained from the velocity of TBA(qct), the bare unitcell tight-binding system reconstructed from the quantum cluster theory frontend, so that the vertices are guaranteed to be consistent with the Green's function. Vertex corrections are neglected (exact in DMFT, an approximation for CPT/VCA). The returned tensors are in units of e²/h (per layer).

  • brillouinzone: the momentum mesh over the full Brillouin zone of the unitcell (fixed-mesh sum replacing the BZ integral).
  • μ: the chemical potential. Following the convention of Ω and expectation, the Green's function is evaluated at ω+μ while the thermal weights and integration limits are expressed in the frequency relative to μ, so the Hamiltonian needs not contain the -μN term; leave μ=0 when it already does. (Within the fixed quantum-number sector of the ED solvers this shift is exact even at finite temperature, since -μN is then a constant of the sector.)
  • β: the inverse temperature; Inf for zero temperature (then -∂f becomes δ(ω) and f becomes θ(-ω)).
  • η: the Lorentzian broadening of the Green's function (plays the role of a phenomenological scattering rate).
  • which: which part(s) of the conductivity to compute, :surf, :sea or :both.

Run-time options (passed at the assignment call site, see options):

  • gauge (default :rcoordinate): the gauge of the velocity vertices; the default is the gauge of the periodized Green's function of QCT.
  • rtol (default 1e-6), atol (default 0.0): relative/absolute tolerance of the adaptive quadrature (quadgk). The ω integrals are evaluated by quadgk over the infinite interval ((-Inf, 0) for the sea term at zero temperature, (-Inf, Inf) otherwise), with the momentum-mesh sum inside the integrand; the impurity solver caches its Green's function (and its inverse) at the latest frequency, so it is evaluated only once per quadrature node and shared across all momenta.

TightBindingApproximation.TBA — Method
TBA(qct::Union{QCT, Algorithm{<:QCT}}) -> TBA

The bare tight-binding system on the unitcell of a quantum cluster theory, reconstructed from the physical quadratic terms and the Hilbert space carried by its perturbation, so that consistency with the Green's function is guaranteed by construction. Its Bloch Hamiltonian is the bare band structure h₀(k), and its velocity vertices (velocity) enter e.g. the Conductivity task.

The Weiss fields of VCA frontend are excluded. An error is thrown for a frontend constructed from pre-computed operators (e.g., from Wannier90), whose perturbation carries no terms.

QuantumClusterTheories.chemicalpotential — Method
chemicalpotential(qct::Algorithm{<:QCT}, n::Real; kwargs...) -> Float64
chemicalpotential(
    qct::QCT, n::Real;
    bracket::Union{Nothing, Tuple{Real, Real}}=nothing, xatol::Real=1e-6, xrtol::Real=1e-6,
    brillouinzone::BrillouinZone=BrillouinZone(qct.lattice, 100), atol::Real=1e-6, rtol::Real=1e-6, maxevals::Int=10^6
) -> Float64

Find the chemical potential μ at which the filling of a quantum cluster theory system is n, by a bracketed root search (Brent's method) on the monotone function filling(qct; μ)-n.

The returned chemical potential is additional to any chemical potential term already present in the Hamiltonian (see filling). Since the filling is monotone non-decreasing in μ, the search is globally convergent; when n sits on a gapped plateau, a value within the plateau is returned, which is physically correct as the chemical potential is not uniquely determined inside a gap. The frozen-eigenstate caveat of filling for BdG systems applies to the returned value as well. The bracket is found automatically by interval doubling unless explicitly provided. xatol and xrtol control the tolerance on μ, while the remaining keywords are passed to filling.

QuantumClusterTheories.filling — Method
filling(qct::Algorithm{<:QCT}; kwargs...) -> Float64
filling(
    qct::QCT;
    brillouinzone::BrillouinZone=BrillouinZone(qct.lattice, 100), μ::Real=0.0, atol::Real=1e-6, rtol::Real=1e-6, maxevals::Int=10^6
) -> Float64

Compute the filling (particle number per lattice site, summed over spins and orbitals) of a quantum cluster theory system at the given chemical potential μ. For superconducting (BdG) systems, only the particle (annihilation) sector of the Nambu space is counted, so no double counting occurs.

The chemical potential enters by shifting the frequency argument of the impurity solver's Green's function, so it is additional to any chemical potential term already present in the Hamiltonian, and changing it does not require re-diagonalization.

Within the fixed quantum-number sector of the impurity solver, this shift is exact for normal systems: the particle number is a good quantum number, so -μN is a constant of each sector and only translates the excitation energies without touching the eigenstates. For superconducting (BdG) systems, however, the particle number fluctuates, and the shift amounts to a frozen-eigenstate approximation: the quasiparticle energies are translated while the condensate is kept fixed, although the true eigenstate would readjust with μ. In this case, the chemical potential should be put in the Hamiltonian (e.g. an Onsite(:μ, ...) term) and the impurity solver re-diagonalized by update! instead.
